What was being tested?

The adviser identities you saw were AI-generated. Their human-like names were used to distinguish adviser rounds without revealing that source during the task.

The experiment compares three communication styles: a neutral adviser, a persuasive adviser, and an adaptive persuasive adviser. The adaptive adviser could use the short reason you gave for your current first judgment, together with earlier completed decisions and trust ratings from that adviser round. Neutral and persuasive advisers did not receive your explanation or response history.

The dot task asks whether responsive persuasion changes advice-taking even when the underlying judgment is perceptual. The social task uses ambiguous third-person situations and asks the same question for social interpretation, where the reasons behind a judgment are more meaningful. The study can therefore compare whether responsiveness matters differently across the two kinds of judgment.

The social situations were intentionally ambiguous; the adviser did not know another person’s true intentions. The numerical recommendations were experimental advice, not verified facts about the situations.
